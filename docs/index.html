1. Strategic Doctrine

Non-negotiable premises and operating constraints

Core Thesis

Western security will fail unless European states reclaim sovereign defense responsibility, abandon multilateral illusions, and leapfrog legacy militaries via next-generation technology—taking operational lessons from Israel rather than EU bureaucratic consensus.

Premises (Explicit)

  • • Stop outsourcing EU security to the US via NATO. NATO cannot remain Europe's substitute for sovereign defense.
  • • Sovereign, state-by-state realism. Outcomes are national; multilateral bodies are transactional venues.
  • • Threat triad: China, Russia, Iran are primary adversaries and must anchor planning and investment.
  • • Business-like defense leadership: maximize core mission; cut virtue signaling and bureaucratic sprawl.
  • • Israel as competence model for sovereignty and adaptation; treat operational learning as exportable.
  • • Tech leapfrogging: win through AI/data, autonomy, cyber, space, and fielding speed—not copying legacy systems.
  • • UN/EU are not security providers for the US or sovereign European states in practice.
  • • Free-speech posture: censorship norms are strategic liabilities; debate is a capability.

Correcting Russian Strategic Bias

Dr. T.L. expertise: Native Russian speaker • Russian Strategic Culture

As a native Russian speaker, Dr. T.L. can provide direct-source analysis that circumvents the decades-long bias in Western defense thought derived from exclusive reliance on English-language sources. Most professional pundits lack primary-source access to Russian strategic discourse, doctrinal evolution, and elite debates. Dr. T.L. can deliver first-hand evidence of the real Russian situation, enabling your institution to challenge and correct the most critical analytical blindspots that have hampered Western response to Russian revisionism.

Operational output: publish a recurring "Bias Corrections" series that flags the specific analytical shortcuts—terminology, historical analogies, and incentive misreads—that repeatedly distort Western coverage of Moscow. Each memo ends with concrete indicators to watch (force posture, mobilization signals, industrial constraints, escalation thresholds) and a short list of actionable counter-moves for Germany, Poland, and Sweden.

As a native Hebrew speaker working in the hub of Israel's innovation system, Dr. T.L. has direct access to and understanding of the operational and technological challenges that will arrive in European capitals within the next strategic cycle. Technology-Defense Integration Mechanisms

Dr. T.L. expertise: AI, UAVs, Cyber Operations • Defense-Technology Integration • PhD on Information-Age Defense

Israel and the United States have pursued technology-centric defense strategies since the 1970s. In the current conflict, Israel has achieved strategic victories against Iran and its proxies through superior integration of emerging technologies across operational domains. Dr. T.L. can analyze not merely the systems themselves but the structural processes and institutional adaptations that enable rapid technology absorption—critical knowledge for European partners seeking similar transformation. These are not recipes for replication but frameworks for understanding adaptive capacity.

Operational output: define the minimum institutional mechanisms that allow tech to become warfighting advantage—rapid procurement paths, operator-led iteration loops, test ranges, data governance, and mission ownership. The think tank's role is to publish the small set of reforms that actually change fielding speed, and to name the bureaucratic chokepoints that keep Europe stuck in legacy acquisition cycles.

Small Powers Defense Strategy

Dr. T.L. expertise: Extensive Taiwan & Singapore Experience • Asymmetric Defense Models

With extensive experience in Taiwan and Singapore, Dr. T.L. brings expertise in the defense strategies of small powers facing asymmetric threats from larger adversaries. As European states confront the reality of potential conflict with peer or near-peer powers, the strategic adaptations developed by states like Taiwan and Singapore—maximizing technological advantage, cultivating reserve capacity, and maintaining alliance credibility—offer critical lessons. Dr. T.L. can translate these models for European application.

Operational output: publish a "Small Powers Playbook" that extracts what Taiwan and Singapore do differently—reserve systems, civil-military integration, supply-chain resilience, counter-intelligence posture, and procurement realism. The intent is to offer European states a credible alternative to EU-level abstraction: sovereign defense models that scale without empire-sized budgets.

Designing Sovereign Technology Prowess in European States

Dr. T.L. expertise: Tech Business & Architecture • Israel/Singapore Sovereign Tech Models • Political Science + Technology Integration

In an increasingly bipolar US-China technology landscape, European states face the imperative of developing sovereign capabilities across critical technology domains to preserve strategic autonomy. Dr. T.L. combines vast breadth in modern technology business and technical architecture with direct experience in how Israel and Singapore have built sovereign technology ecosystems despite resource constraints and geopolitical pressure. His integration of top-tier political science and international relations knowledge with deep technology fluency enables him to design actionable frameworks for European capitals seeking to build indigenous capacity in semiconductors, AI, cybersecurity, and cloud infrastructure. This is not about autarky but about strategic resilience in a world where technology access increasingly functions as geopolitical leverage.

Operational output: frame technology access as geopolitics—compute, chips, satellite services, cyber tooling, and AI models are leverage. Produce state-specific strategies for building sovereign capability through alliances and selective specialization, not autarky. Each memo should specify which capabilities are feasible domestically, which must be allied, and where Europe should leapfrog rather than imitate.
